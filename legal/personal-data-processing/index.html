Personal Data Processing Policy

This document sets out, in operational detail, how personal data is processed within Pulse Call: the principles we follow, the records we keep, the retention schedule we apply and the procedures that support the rights described in our Privacy Policy.

How this differs from the Privacy Policy

The Privacy Policy tells you, as a visitor or candidate, what happens to your data. This document is the internal processing standard behind it: lawful bases in detail, retention rules, processor requirements, breach handling and request procedures. Both are published so that our commitments can be checked.

1. Purpose and scope

This policy applies to all processing of personal data carried out by SEAWAY DRAGON LP (registration number LP020855, registered in the United Kingdom on 17 February 2020, registered office Suite 6032, 128 Aldersgate Street, Barbican, London, England, EC1A 4AE) in connection with recruitment through the Pulse Call website and related channels.

It binds all employees, contractors and managers who handle candidate data, in every country in which we recruit. Processors act under contract on the terms set out in section 10.

The applicable law is the UK GDPR together with the Data Protection Act 2018 and, where we offer roles to people located in the European Union, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 by virtue of its Article 3(2).

5. Lawful bases in detail

Pre-contractual steps — Article 6(1)(b)

Assessing an application is a step taken at the candidate's request before entering into a contract. This basis covers contacting the candidate, holding an interview, recording assessment notes and communicating the outcome. It ends when the process ends.

Consent — Article 6(1)(a)

Keeping an unsuccessful application on file for future roles goes beyond the current process, so it needs separate, freely given consent. Consent is requested as a distinct, unticked option; refusing it has no effect on the current application.

Legitimate interests — Article 6(1)(f)

Used for the referral programme, for site security and for spam filtering. Before relying on this basis we carry out and document a three-part assessment: whether the interest is legitimate, whether the processing is necessary to achieve it, and whether it is overridden by the rights of the individual. Referral processing is limited to a single contact attempt if the person does not respond.

Legal obligation — Article 6(1)(c)

Retention of payment and accounting records relating to referral bonuses and salaries.

6. Special category and criminal offence data

We do not seek special category data under Article 9 or criminal offence data under Article 10, and no processing condition in Schedule 1 of the Data Protection Act 2018 is relied upon.

If such data reaches us anyway

Where a candidate volunteers special category data in the free-text field, the recruiter must delete it from the record without transferring it elsewhere and without taking it into account in the assessment. The deletion is logged. No copy is kept.

7. Consent management

  • Consent is requested separately from the terms of the application, in plain language, with no pre-ticked boxes.
  • We record what the person was told, what they agreed to and when.
  • Withdrawal is as easy as giving consent: one message to hr@pulse-call.com.
  • On withdrawal we stop the relevant processing and delete the data unless another basis genuinely applies, in which case we explain which.
  • Cookie consent is collected through the banner, stored with a record of the choice, and can be changed at any time.

9. Deletion and anonymisation

At the end of a retention period the record is deleted from the CRM and from any working copies, including exports and message threads. Where statistics are needed, data is anonymised so that re-identification is not reasonably possible, and aggregated figures may then be kept indefinitely.

Retention periods are reviewed on a rolling basis at least quarterly, and deletion is logged so that it can be evidenced.

11. International transfers

Transfers outside the UK or the EEA take place only where one of the Chapter V mechanisms is in place: an applicable adequacy decision; the EU Standard Contractual Clauses; the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs; or another appropriate safeguard.

Where SCCs or the IDTA are used, we carry out and document a transfer risk assessment covering the law and practice of the destination country, and we apply supplementary measures where the assessment requires them.

13. Personal data breaches

A personal data breach is any security incident leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data.

  1. Report internally without delay. Anyone who becomes aware of a suspected breach reports it to hr@pulse-call.com immediately, without attempting to resolve it alone.
  2. Contain and assess. We stop the ongoing exposure, establish what data and how many people are affected, and assess the risk to their rights.
  3. Notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk. If we cannot provide full details in time, we notify in phases.
  4. Notify affected individuals without undue delay where the risk to their rights is high, in clear language, describing the likely consequences and the steps we have taken.
  5. Record. Every breach is logged with its facts, effects and remedial action, whether or not it was notifiable.

14. Handling data subject requests

  • Requests may arrive in any form and through any channel; there is no obligation on the individual to use a template.
  • Any staff member receiving one forwards it to hr@pulse-call.com on the same working day, because the clock starts on receipt by the organisation.
  • We respond within one month, extendable by two further months for complex or numerous requests, with reasons given inside the first month.
  • Identity is verified only where there is genuine doubt, using the least intrusive method available.
  • Responses are free of charge; a reasonable fee or refusal is possible only for manifestly unfounded or excessive requests, and we explain the reasoning and the right to complain.
  • Where data about the requester also concerns another person, we release it only if that is reasonable, redacting where necessary.

15. Records of processing

We maintain a record of processing activities in line with Article 30, covering purposes, categories of data subjects and data, recipients, transfers, retention periods and security measures. The record is updated whenever a new tool, purpose or recipient is introduced, and is made available to a supervisory authority on request.

16. Impact assessments

A Data Protection Impact Assessment is carried out before any processing likely to result in a high risk to individuals — for example large-scale profiling, automated decision-making with significant effects, or systematic monitoring. Current recruitment processing does not meet that threshold, and this conclusion is reviewed whenever the processing changes.
